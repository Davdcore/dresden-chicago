# Lobbster - Divine Character Sheet

## Identity

| Detail | Description |
|---|---|
| Name | Lobbster |
| Nature | Tennis fortune god |
| Domains | Sportsmanship, sportive faith, fair play, and the luck of a close point |
| Emblem | A golden tennis ball resting on a white chalk line |
| Common saying | "Play the point you have, not the one you deserve." |
| Status | Belief and stories are known; divine existence is unproven |

## Character

Lobbster is said to care less about who wins than how the game is played. His sportive faith is not certainty that a favored player will win; it is the trust that a match is worth playing honestly, even when the score turns against you. He favors humility, effort, respect for an opponent, and the courage to continue after an embarrassing mistake.

Lobbster is not believed to punish every boast or reward every underdog. In the stories told about him, fortune creates an opening; the players decide what to do with it. A gust can ruin a serve, but it cannot force a player to become gracious or spiteful.

## Signs and Influence

- A sudden change in wind during a serve or toss.
- A ball clipping the net and landing on an unexpected side.
- A brief calm that lets a nervous player reset.
- A chalk line that appears unusually bright in the sun.

Any one sign can be ordinary chance. A pattern may be taken as a sign by believers, but Lobbster never provides a public explanation.

## Linked Event

**[The club match](../wiki.md#the-tennis-und-sauna-club-schboinktown-match)** is the best-known possible sign of Lobbster's presence in this story. The wind in the trees repeatedly troubles Tobias's serve, then falls still as Mara settles into her game; dappled light shifts over the court, and an unlikely net-cord point deepens the suspicion. If Lobbster intervened, he did not hand Mara the match: she still had to return the ball, stay composed, and win the points.

## Limits

Lobbster cannot be shown to have caused the match's outcome. The weather, Tobias's mistakes, and Mara's unusually focused play all have ordinary explanations. Whether he answered someone's unspoken hope, upheld the spirit of fair play, or was never there at all remains open.
